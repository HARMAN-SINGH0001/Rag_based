Mahatma Gandhi, whose full name was Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, was one of the greatest leaders in the history of India. He was born on 2 October 1869 in Porbandar, a small town in the state of Gujarat. Gandhi is widely known as the “Father of the Nation” in India because of his important role in the Indian freedom struggle. He believed strongly in the principles of truth and non-violence, which he called **Satyagraha**. Through peaceful protests and civil disobedience, he inspired millions of Indians to stand against the rule of the United Kingdom over India.

Gandhi studied law in London and later worked in South Africa, where he first began fighting against racial discrimination. His experiences there shaped his ideas about justice and equality. When he returned to India, he led several important movements such as the Non-Cooperation Movement, the Salt March, and the Quit India Movement. These movements encouraged people to protest peacefully and refuse to cooperate with British authorities.

Gandhi lived a very simple life and promoted values like self-reliance, honesty, and unity among people of different religions and communities. He also encouraged the use of **khadi**, a hand-spun cloth, as a symbol of independence and economic freedom. His teachings influenced many leaders around the world, including Martin Luther King Jr. and Nelson Mandela.

Sadly, Mahatma Gandhi was assassinated on 30 January 1948, but his ideas of peace, truth, and non-violence continue to inspire people across the world. His life remains a powerful example of how courage and moral values can bring about great social and political change.
